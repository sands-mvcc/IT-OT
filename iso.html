ISO 27001 Framework Overview

What is ISO 27001?

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is the world's best-known standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). It provides a systematic approach to managing sensitive company information to ensure it remains secure.

Core Principles (CIA Triad)

🔒 Confidentiality

Ensuring information is accessible only to authorized individuals

🛡️ Integrity

Maintaining accuracy and completeness of information and processing methods

📊 Availability

Ensuring authorized users have access to information when needed

Key Benefits

  • Reduces vulnerability to cyber-attacks
  • Demonstrates commitment to information security
  • Helps meet regulatory compliance requirements
  • Builds trust with customers and stakeholders
  • Provides competitive advantage in global markets
  • Enables systematic risk management

Did You Know?

Over 70,000 ISO 27001 certificates have been issued globally across 150 countries!

• IT industry has the largest number of certified enterprises (~20%)

• The standard is applicable to organizations of any size and sector

• Certificates are valid for 3 years with annual surveillance audits

PDCA Cycle in ISO 27001

The Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle is the foundation of ISO 27001, ensuring continuous improvement of the Information Security Management System (ISMS).

📋 PLAN

Establish ISMS scope, policies, objectives, and risk treatment plans

⚙️ DO

Implement security controls, training programs, and operational procedures

🔍 CHECK

Monitor, audit, and evaluate ISMS performance and effectiveness

🔄 ACT

Take corrective actions and implement continuous improvements

PDCA Mapping to ISO 27001 Clauses

Plan Phase (Clauses 4-6)

Context of organization, leadership commitment, planning for ISMS

Do Phase (Clauses 7-8)

Support resources, competence, awareness, and operational controls

Check Phase (Clause 9)

Performance evaluation, monitoring, internal audit, management review

Act Phase (Clause 10)

Continuous improvement, nonconformity and corrective action

Risk Management & Continuous Improvement

Risk Assessment Process

Step 1: Risk Identification

Identify information security risks related to threats, vulnerabilities, and potential impacts

Step 2: Risk Analysis

Evaluate the likelihood and consequences of identified risks

Step 3: Risk Evaluation

Compare analyzed risks against risk acceptance criteria

Step 4: Risk Treatment

Select and implement appropriate risk treatment options

Risk Treatment Options

✅ Accept

Acknowledge risk and accept the potential impact

🚫 Avoid

Eliminate the source of risk or discontinue activity

📉 Reduce

Implement controls to mitigate likelihood or impact

🔄 Transfer

Share risk through insurance or outsourcing

Continuous Improvement Principles

  • Regular monitoring and measurement of ISMS performance
  • Periodic internal audits and management reviews
  • Corrective and preventive actions based on findings
  • Adaptation to changing threat landscape and business needs

Annex A Controls & Component Requirements

ISO 27001:2022 Annex A contains 93 security controls organized into 4 main themes. Organizations select applicable controls based on their risk assessment.

Control Categories (93 Total Controls)

🏢 Organizational (37 controls)

Policies, procedures, roles, responsibilities, and governance structures

👥 People (8 controls)

HR security, training, awareness, and disciplinary processes

🏠 Physical (14 controls)

Physical security, environmental protection, and secure disposal

💻 Technological (34 controls)

Access control, cryptography, network security, and system maintenance

Key New Controls in 2022 Version

5.7 Threat Intelligence

Collection and analysis of information security threat intelligence

5.23 Information Security for Cloud Services

Define and monitor information security when using cloud services

8.9 Configuration Management

Maintain security configurations of hardware, software, and services

8.10 Information Deletion

Securely delete information when no longer required

Statement of Applicability (SoA)

Organizations must create an SoA documenting which Annex A controls they implement and justify any exclusions.

Control 5.1 - Information Security Policy
Status: Implemented
Implementation: Company has established comprehensive information security policy approved by senior management and reviewed annually.
Justification: Required for all organizations to provide direction and support for information security.

Certification Framework & Levels

ISO 27001 Certification Process

Pre-Certification: Gap Analysis

Assess current state against ISO 27001 requirements and identify gaps

ISMS Implementation

Develop and implement policies, procedures, and controls based on risk assessment

Internal Audit

Conduct internal ISMS audit to verify implementation and effectiveness

Management Review

Senior management reviews ISMS performance and authorizes certification audit

Stage 1 Audit (Documentation Review)

External auditor reviews ISMS documentation and readiness for Stage 2

Stage 2 Audit (Implementation Assessment)

External auditor verifies ISMS implementation and effectiveness

Certificate Issuance

If successful, certification body issues 3-year ISO 27001 certificate

Surveillance Audits

Annual surveillance audits to maintain certification validity

Certification Scope Considerations

📍 Physical Locations

Define which offices, data centers, or facilities are included

🎯 Business Units

Specify which departments, divisions, or functions are covered

💾 Information Assets

Identify types of information and systems within scope

🔗 Third Parties

Consider suppliers, partners, and outsourced services

Certification Body Requirements

  • Must be accredited by national accreditation body
  • Auditors must have appropriate qualifications and experience
  • Certification follows ISO/IEC 17021 and ISO/IEC 27006 standards
  • Certificates are internationally recognized when properly accredited

Industrial Automation & Control Systems Security

While ISO 27001 provides general ISMS requirements, industrial control systems require specialized considerations. ISO/IEC TR 27019 provides specific guidance for energy utility process control systems.

Operational Technology (OT) vs Information Technology (IT)

🏭 OT Characteristics

Real-time operations, safety-critical systems, legacy equipment, air-gapped networks

💻 IT Characteristics

Data processing, enterprise applications, regular updates, internet connectivity

Zone and Conduit Model

Security Zones

Logical groupings of assets with similar security requirements and risk profiles

  • Enterprise Zone (Corporate IT)
  • DMZ (Demilitarized Zone)
  • Control Zone (SCADA/HMI)
  • Safety Zone (Emergency systems)

Security Conduits

Secure communication channels between zones with defined security controls

  • Firewalls and network segmentation
  • VPN tunnels for remote access
  • Data diodes for one-way communication
  • Protocol filtering and deep packet inspection

Industrial Security Considerations

⚡ Real-time Requirements

Security controls must not interfere with time-critical operations

🔧 Legacy Systems

Many industrial systems lack modern security features

🛡️ Safety vs Security

Safety systems may need to override security controls

🔌 Air-gapped Networks

Physical isolation from enterprise networks and internet

Integration with Other Standards

Complementary Standards for Industrial Systems

  • IEC 62443: Industrial communication networks - Network and system security
  • NIST SP 800-82: Guide to Industrial Control Systems (ICS) Security
  • ISO/IEC 27019: Information security for energy utility industry
  • ISA-84: Safety Instrumented Systems standard
