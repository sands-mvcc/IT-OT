Operating Principles

Pneumatic cylinders convert potential energy of compressed gas into mechanical linear force. Gas compressibility creates unique dynamic stiffness and velocity behavior.

Key Instructional Concepts

  1. Meter-Out Speed Control: Throttling exhaust air (rather than inlet air) creates an opposing back-pressure cushion that prevents lunging, stick-slip, and erratic motion under varying loads.
  2. Differential Force Output: Due to rod area reduction ($A_{rod} < A_{cap}$), extension force is higher than retraction force at identical supply pressure ($F_{ext} = P \cdot A_{cap}$, $F_{ret} = P \cdot A_{rod}$).
  3. End-Position Cushioning: Trapping a volume of air in a small cushion pocket near end-of-stroke forces exhaust through a needle valve, absorbing kinetic energy smoothly ($E_k = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$).
